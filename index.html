The Pomodoro Technique

A time-management method that breaks work into short, focused intervals separated by deliberate breaks — so attention stays sharp and effort stays sustainable.

It was developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s and named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer he used as a student — pomodoro is Italian for "tomato." Each focused interval is itself called a pomodoro.

How it works

  1. Pick a single task to work on.
  2. Start a 25-minute focus session and work on it without interruptions until the timer ends.
  3. Take a 5-minute short break to step away and recharge.
  4. After four focus sessions, take a longer 15–30 minute break, then begin a fresh set.

Why it works

A fixed interval makes starting feel manageable and gives your attention a clear boundary to defend against distractions. Regular breaks keep mental energy from draining, and counting completed sessions turns vague effort into visible, satisfying progress.

In Cadence

The four stars beneath the timer fill as you finish focus sessions and reset after a long break, while your streak plant stays healthy as long as you keep the habit alive. You can tailor session and break lengths anytime under Settings → Focus.
